## 1. Houses (bhavas)

- Houses, called bhavas in Sanskrit, are twelve divisions of the celestial sphere fixed relative to the Earth's horizon. Bhava means "state of being" or "condition of existence".
- Planets move through the zodiac; houses provide the framework through which their energies are expressed in distinct areas of life.
- Houses are calculated from the Ascendant (Lagna), the sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth. It marks the beginning of the first house and anchors the chart, making it highly personal.
- The horoscope thus becomes a map of the arenas of growth, challenge and fulfilment in a life.

## 2. Houses versus signs

| Signs (rasis) | Houses (bhavas) |
|---|---|
| Fixed divisions of the zodiac, each spanning 30° | Divisions of the chart based on the Ascendant |
| Constant and universal, always in the same order (Aries to Pisces) | Relative: they shift according to the rising sign at birth |
| Show qualities and characteristics | Represent domains of life |
| Fixed, based on the Sun's annual path | Rotate with the Earth's 24-hour cycle, changing with time and place of birth |
| A planet in a sign expresses its nature through that sign's qualities | A planet in a house expresses its influence in that area of life |
| Example: Mars in Cancer takes on watery, emotional qualities | Example: Mars in the 4th house influences home, mother, property and inner peace |

In the natural zodiac (Kalapurusha Kundli), Aries is the 1st house, Taurus the 2nd, and so on to Pisces as the 12th.

## 3. House systems

| System | Method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Equal House | Each house spans exactly 30° from the Ascendant | The default in Vedic astrology. Simple, preserves symbolic meanings, works at all latitudes. Used by traditional Vedic astrologers and the Parashara system |
| Porphyry | The Ascendant is used as the midpoint, with 15° added before and after it | As described in the notes |
| Placidus | Houses based on the time degrees take to rise | Western astrology. Creates unequal houses reflecting temporal experience. Psychologically accurate but fails at extreme latitudes |
| Whole Sign | Each sign becomes a house; the rising sign is the 1st house | Used in ancient Hellenistic and Vedic traditions. Extremely simple and historically authentic |

Why the Equal House system is used in Vedic astrology:
- It maintains the 30° division that matches the rasi system.
- It preserves symbolic meanings without distortion.
- It aligns with Parashara's principles of house signification.
- It reflects the orderly, mathematical nature of cosmic law (Rita).

Practical advantages:
- Works at all latitudes, including polar regions.
- Simple enough for manual computation.
- Gives consistent results across astrologers.
- Integrates seamlessly with the Vimshottari Dasha system.

## 6. Purusharthas — the four goals of life

Almost all human actions are driven by some form of desire. The purusharthas — Dharma, Artha, Kama and Moksha — are the four fundamental aims of life, each a distinct type of desire or motivation.

The twelve houses fall into four sets, each reflecting one of the purusharthas.

| Purushartha | Houses |
|---|---|
| Dharma | 1, 5, 9 |
| Artha | 2, 6, 10 |
| Kama | 3, 7, 11 |
| Moksha | 4, 8, 12 |

### 7.1 Kendra houses (1, 4, 7, 10)
- The bhavas in quadrants, 90° apart starting from the first, are the angular or cardinal houses: Kendras.
- The angles — Ascendant, Midheaven (Medium Coeli), Descendant and Nadir (Imum Coeli) — are the most potent and influential areas of any chart. They are the "cardinal points" and relate to the directions east, south, west and north. "Cardinal" comes from Latin cardo, "hinge": something of central significance.
- A planet in a Kendra performs more powerfully.
- These are the four main pillars of life: the basic needs and foundations on which life is centred.
- Their ruling deity is Lord Vishnu, Lord of Sustenance, so they are called Vishnu Sthanas and show the stability of a person's life.
- Malefic planets placed in Kendras do well.

### 7.2 Panapara houses (2, 5, 8, 11)
- Succedent houses are so named because planets in them succeed, by the daily revolution of the sky, to the cardinal position.
- Planets here have mediocre strength.
- Panapara relates to money: the houses of accumulation.

### 7.3 Apoklima houses (3, 6, 9, 12)
- The last house of each quadrant is cadent. Its strength declines compared with the first two houses of the quadrant, and planets here are not very powerful.
- Apoklima is the Greek name, meaning "falling" or "decline". The notes give the Vedic word as Aya-klema: aya ("income") + klema ("weakness, becoming less") = "expense of wealth".
- Each is the 12th (house of expense) from the house that follows it:
  - 3rd is 12th from the 4th: money spent on home and family.
  - 6th is 12th from the 7th: money spent on the spouse.
  - 9th is 12th from the 10th: money spent on father and higher learning.
  - 12th is 12th from the 1st: money spent on oneself.
- The 9th is the best of them: as a Dharma house, planets there give auspicious results and tend to bring good luck.
- The 12th is the weakest: energy cannot be expressed at the material level, but it is good for spiritual progress.
- The 9th, being 12th from the house signifying father, indicates a special debt: Pitr Rina, the debt to one's parents for raising us, repaid by looking after them.

Source note: the heading for this section lists the houses as "3, 6, 9, 11"; the text gives 3, 6, 9 and 12.

### 7.4 Trikona houses (1, 5, 9)
- The bhavas in a triangle, 120° apart starting from the first, are the trines or Trikonas: the spiritual triangle.
- They are the most powerful and auspicious bhavas, representing dharma, past merit and fortune. Benefics here give excellent results.
- Well-placed Trikona bhavas and lords give abundant divine grace and guidance through good and bad times, and bless one with righteousness. They hold the soul's mission, accumulated merit and the manifestation of past karma.
- Their ruling deity is Goddess Lakshmi, so they are called Lakshmi Sthanas.
- Vishnu and Lakshmi are husband and wife, and their connection in a birth chart is highly desirable: the union of a Vishnu Sthana and a Lakshmi Sthana produces Raja Yoga. A planet ruling both is a Yogakaraka.

### 7.5 Upachaya houses (3, 6, 10, 11)
- Houses of improvement and growth over time through effort and learning. Results improve with age.
- Malefic planets (Sun, Mars, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu) perform especially well here.

### 7.6 Dusthana (Trika) houses (6, 8, 12)
- Difficult houses representing obstacles, debts, hidden issues and liberation: houses of challenge and transformation.
- They show areas of struggle but also hold potential for spiritual growth and detachment.
- Benefics here soften difficulties, while malefics intensify challenges.
- Malefics can nevertheless perform well here: Mars in the 6th defeats enemies.
- Generally, benefics placed here are not favourable.

### 7.7 Maraka houses (2, 7)
- Examined in longevity analysis. Their effects activate only during specific Dasha and Bhukti periods, with multiple confirming factors.
- The 3rd and 8th are the houses of Jeeva Shakti (life force). The 12th from any house is the loss of that house. The 2nd (12th from the 3rd) and the 7th (12th from the 8th) are therefore the Maraka, or killer, houses.

### 7.8 Houses and the stages of life
- Kendras denote childhood, Panaparas middle age and Apoklimas old age.
- Houses 1 to 8 relate to one's marital life; 9 to 11 to religious and moral belief; the 12th to the other world, moksha.

## 8. House lordship (Adhipati)

- Every house has a lord: the planet ruling the sign that occupies it. Lordship is the key to reading a chart, as it connects the fixed framework of the houses with the moving energies of the planets.
- Signs and lords: each sign has a planetary ruler — Aries by Mars, Taurus by Venus, Gemini by Mercury, and so on.
- Houses and lords: when a house falls in a sign, the ruler of that sign becomes the lord of that house. Example: if Leo is the 10th house, the Sun is the 10th lord.
- The house lord carries responsibility for that area of life. Its condition, placement and strength show how smoothly or with what difficulty that domain unfolds. In the example, the Sun's position elsewhere in the chart tells much about career, reputation and authority.

## 9. Dispositors

- A dispositor is the planet that rules the sign in which another planet is placed.
- Example: Venus in Aries. The lord of Aries is Mars, so the dispositor of Venus is Mars. (The illustration uses a Libra Ascendant with Venus in Aries, the 7th house.)

## 10. House-to-house exchange (Parivartana Yoga)

- If two sign lords exchange positions and occupy each other's house, Parivartana (Sthana Parivartana) Yoga is formed: the lord of house A is in house B, and the lord of house B is in house A.
- A planet is powerful in its own house. When two planets exchange, each gains the strength of the other; energy moving between the houses is enhanced and both houses become stronger.
- Example — Aquarius Ascendant: Jupiter in Taurus (4th house) and Venus in Sagittarius (11th house). The 11th lord Jupiter is in the 4th, and the 4th lord Venus is in the 11th.

## 11. Nature of planets due to lordship

Two factors form the foundation of interpretation:
- Natural (Naisargika) nature: how a planet influences character, behaviour, skills and attitudes.
- Functional nature, determined by house lordship: the kinds of circumstances and situations a person meets in life. It varies with the Ascendant, depending on the houses a planet owns from the Lagna.

| Classification | Groups |
|---|---|
| Natural (permanent) | Natural benefics (Shubha Grahas); natural malefics (Ashubha or Papa Grahas) |
| Temporal (functional) | Kendra lords (1, 4, 7, 10); Trikona lords (1, 5, 9); Trishadaya lords (3, 6, 11); Parashraya lords (12, 2, 8) |

### 11.1 Natural benefics and malefics
- Natural benefics: full Moon, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus — stronger in beneficence in that ascending order. Jupiter and Venus are first in giving auspicious effects.
- Natural malefics: Sun, Saturn, Mars — stronger in maleficence in that ascending order.
- Moon: called semi-benefic. As worded in the notes, she is auspicious from the full Moon to the 7th tithi; from the 8th tithi her auspiciousness decreases until she is entirely malefic on Amavasya; thereafter her inauspiciousness decreases steadily, vanishing by the 7th tithi of Shukla Paksha, when her beneficence begins again.
- Mercury: neutral — benefic when associated with a benefic and malefic when associated with a malefic. Alone in a house, he is favourable.

### 11.2 Kendra and Trikona lordship
1. Ascendant lord: the 1st house is both a Kendra and a Trikona, so its lord always gives auspicious effects, whether a natural benefic or malefic, and its effects carry special significance.
2. Kendra lords:
   - Natural benefics do not give auspicious (Shubha Yoga) effects as lords of Kendras. They lose part of their beneficence but remain benefics.
   - Natural malefics do not give inauspiciousness as lords of Kendras. They lose part of their maleficence but remain malefics; this does not make them auspicious. To be auspicious by Kendra lordship, a malefic must also own a Trikona.
3. Trikona lords: both natural benefics and natural malefics give auspicious effects as lords of Trikonas. Trikonas are the houses of Lakshmi.
4. Relationship of Kendra and Trikona lords — of great significance in giving elevation in life:
   - Exchange: the Kendra lord occupies a Trikona and the Trikona lord occupies a Kendra.
   - Conjunction: a Kendra lord and a Trikona lord are together in a Kendra or a Trikona.
   - A Trikona lord in a Kendra gives auspiciousness, even though it is not his own house.
   - A Kendra lord in a Trikona gives auspiciousness, even though it is not his own house.
   - Aspect: the Kendra lord and the Trikona lord aspect each other, each placed in a benefic house.
- A Kendra lord placed in a Dusthana can cause Duryoga (an inauspicious combination).
- Kendras are houses of Vishnu and Trikonas houses of Lakshmi: Vishnu sustains, Lakshmi gives the material benefits (money, power, name).

### 11.3 Yogakaraka
- If one planet is lord of both a Trikona and a Kendra, and occupies a Kendra or a Trikona, it is a Yogakaraka.
- A Yogakaraka rules both a Kendra (1, 4, 7, 10) and a Trikona (1, 5, 9). The relationship between Kendra (Vishnu Sthana) and Trikona (Lakshmi Sthana) lords causes Raja Yoga: power, success and fortune.
- Kendra lords give stability and worldly foundation; Trikona lords give dharma, blessings and fortune; the Yogakaraka combines both, giving auspicious results especially in its Dasha or when strongly placed.

| Lagna | Yogakaraka | Rules Trikona | Rules Kendra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taurus | Saturn | 9th | 10th |
| Cancer | Mars | 5th | 10th |
| Leo | Mars | 9th | 4th |
| Libra | Saturn | 5th | 4th |

### 11.4 Kendradhipati Dosha
- A natural benefic ruling a Kendra (1, 4, 7, 10) can lose its auspiciousness: its beneficence is reduced.
- The benefic owner of a Kendra remains a benefic and does not become a malefic. Likewise a malefic owning a Kendra loses maleficence without becoming a benefic.
- Exception: the 1st lord is often exempt, especially if it also owns a Trikona.
- This dosha applies uniquely to the four dual-sign Ascendants: Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius and Pisces. (For these Ascendants all four Kendras are owned by the benefics Mercury and Jupiter.)

### 11.5 Trishadaya lords (3, 6, 11)
- The lords of the 3rd, 6th and 11th are evil and give inauspicious, malefic results.
- If a Kendra lord simultaneously owns a 3-6-11 house, mere association of a Kendra or Trikona lord does not give yoga.

### 11.6 Parashraya lords (12, 2, 8)
- Parashraya means dependence or support: one planet relying on another for strength or effect.
- The auspiciousness or inauspiciousness of the 12th, 2nd and 8th lords depends on their association with other planets.
- Their comparative strength rises in the order 12th, 2nd, 8th.
- The 8th lord is specifically inauspicious, as the 8th is the 12th from the 9th, the house of dharma.
- If the 8th lord also owns a 3-6-11 house he is inauspicious; if he also owns a Trikona he bestows auspiciousness.

### 11.7 Rahu and Ketu
- Rahu and Ketu, the shadowy planets, predominantly give the effects of the lord of the house they occupy, or of the planet with which they are conjoined.
- Placed in a Kendra or a Trikona and conjoined with or aspected by a Kendra lord or Trikona lord, they acquire the quality of a Yogakaraka.

## 12. Bhavat Bhavam

- Bhavat Bhavam means "from a house to the same count of the house": counting from any house as many houses as that house is from the Ascendant.
- A house is examined by treating it as an Ascendant and finding the house that stands to it as it stands to the Ascendant.

| House | Same count from itself | House | Same count from itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st from the 1st | 1st | 7th from the 7th | 1st |
| 2nd from the 2nd | 3rd | 8th from the 8th | 3rd |
| 3rd from the 3rd | 5th | 9th from the 9th | 5th |
| 4th from the 4th | 7th | 10th from the 10th | 7th |
| 5th from the 5th | 9th | 11th from the 11th | 9th |
| 6th from the 6th | 11th | 12th from the 12th | 11th |

Examples:
1. 1st from the 1st = 1st. The 1st house is self: the self is the foundation.
2. 2nd from the 2nd = 3rd. The 2nd is family, wealth, food, speech; the 3rd is siblings, communications, writing, courage. Speech, communications and writing support the accumulation of wealth, or siblings play a role in the family's wealth.
3. 5th from the 5th = 9th. The 5th is children, creativity, mind, love, speculation, mantras; the 9th is bhagya (fortune), higher education, divine blessings, religion. Divine blessings give children; luck gives success in love and speculation; being religious leads to mantra siddhi and recitation.

## 14. Key principles for analysing a house

1. House rasi: the classification and significance of the sign in the house.
2. House lord: the planet ruling that sign. Its placement (by house and by rasi) shows where the house's themes manifest.
3. Occupants: planets placed in the house directly influence its significations.
4. Aspects: planets aspecting the house (drishti) can modify, strengthen or obstruct its results, depending on the nature of the planet and the quality of the aspect.

## 15. D-2 Hora chart (Hora Varga)

This is the traditional Parashara Hora, which uses only Cancer and Leo.

- The Hora chart is the second divisional chart (D-2) described in the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra.
- It relates specifically to wealth, resources, sustenance and prosperity.
- It divides each sign into two halves (horas) of 15°, ruled by the Sun and the Moon.

| Sign type | First half (0°–15°) | Second half (15°–30°) |
|---|---|---|
| Odd signs (Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius, Aquarius) | Sun's hora | Moon's hora |
| Even signs (Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn, Pisces) | Moon's hora | Sun's hora |

In the D-2 chart, planets in the Sun's hora are placed in Leo and planets in the Moon's hora in Cancer.

Interpretation:
- Planets in the Sun's hora: wealth through authority, government, father, dharmic pursuits.
- Planets in the Moon's hora: wealth through nurturing, mother, family, sustenance, emotional support.
- The Hora chart shows how wealth is acquired, sustained or lost, complementing the 2nd house of the main chart.

Key points:
- Sun's hora: Sun, Mars and Jupiter are powerful. Ruled by the Devas.
- Moon's hora: Moon, Venus and Saturn are powerful. Ruled by the Pitris.
- Mercury is powerful in both.
- Strength within a hora: the first part is powerful, the middle medium, and the last part gives nil or minimal results.
