# Horoscope, Signs and Houses — Jyotisha Reference

Scope: the natural zodiac, chart formats, the basis of the 12 signs and their lordships, houses (bhavas), the Ascendant, the birth chart, the Bhava Chalit chart, divisional charts (vargas), the Kalapurusha, and the sign-to-nakshatra map.

Conventions used in this file:
- Longitudes are in degrees from 0° Aries (0°–360°) or as degrees-minutes within a sign.
- "Source note" marks places where the original notes were inconsistent; nothing was silently corrected.

## 1. The natural zodiac, birth chart and horoscope

- The foundation of a horoscope is the astrological chart, based on the natural zodiac in the sky.
- Natural zodiac: the universal framework — the 12 signs and their lordships, the houses and the nakshatras. It is the base.
- Birth chart (natal chart): the individual's unique diagram, mapping the positions of the planets at the exact time and place of birth.
- Horoscope: the interpretation developed from the birth chart.
- "Horoscope" comes from Greek hora ("time") and skopos ("watcher" or "marker"): "marker of the hour".
- The natural zodiac chart is a plan of the sky divided into 12 parts. There are 12 constellations of signs, so each sign is represented on the chart. Each part is called a house, or bhava.

### 1.1 The four layers of a chart
A chart is a superimposed structure of four interlocking layers:
1. Zodiac signs (rasis)
2. Houses (bhavas)
3. Nakshatras (lunar mansions)
4. Planets (grahas)

### 1.2 Two models for casting a horoscope

| Model | Centre | Description | Effect on chart layout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heliocentric | Sun | The Earth and other planets revolve around the Sun. Seen from the north pole, the Earth rotates on its axis and revolves around the Sun counter-clockwise, west to east. From the Earth, the Sun, Moon and planets appear to rise in the east and set in the west. | Some astrologers held that the Earth's motion should be followed, so successive signs are placed anti-clockwise. |
| Geocentric | Earth | The Earth is taken as the centre; the Sun, Moon and planets orbit it from east to west. | Some astrologers, for exactitude, place successive signs clockwise. |

| Format | Region | Derived from | What is fixed | Direction of signs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Signs: AR Aries, TA Taurus, GE Gemini, CN Cancer, LE Leo, VI Virgo, LI Libra, SC Scorpio, SA Sagittarius, CP Capricorn, AQ Aquarius, PI Pisces.

## 2. Signs (rasis) and nakshatras

- The division of the zodiac into 12 signs and 27 nakshatras is the primary framework of Jyotisha. This primary division is called Rasi.
- The zodiac of 12 signs and 27 constellations was prepared by observing the movement of the Sun and Moon in the geocentric model.
- Synodic month: the time from one new moon to the next (or full moon to full moon), about 29 days 12 hours 44 minutes 3 seconds (29.53 days), rounded to 30 days for practical use. In this time the Sun appears to move about 30° — one sign.
- The zodiac is a 360° circle divided into 12 equal sections of 30°: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius, Pisces.
- The Sun appears to move along the ecliptic, crossing the 12 signs in a year and spending one month in each. The sign the Sun occupies at birth is the Sun sign.

### 2.1 Beginning of the zodiac
- The Sun's movement through the zodiac is tied to the seasons. The beginning of the zodiac was fixed at the equinoxes, when day and night are equal.
- Vernal equinox: the Sun enters 0° Aries (start of spring).
- Autumnal equinox: the Sun enters 0° Libra (start of autumn).
- Solstices mark the turning points of the Sun's path: 0° Cancer on the upward journey and 0° Capricorn on the southward journey.
- Hindu astrology adopted the twelve signs with Aries as the starting point, and fixed Ashwini nakshatra at 0° Aries.

### 2.2 Why the planets own the signs they do — Surya Hora and Chandra Hora
- The zodiac is divided into two halves based on the distance of the Sun from the Earth.
- At the summer solstice, the signs nearest the zenith — Cancer and Leo — are the most productive of heat and warmth. They were therefore assigned to the luminaries: Leo to the Sun, Cancer to the Moon.
- Solar half (Surya Hora): the six signs from Leo to Capricorn, in zodiacal order.
- Lunar half (Chandra Hora): the six signs from Cancer to Aquarius, in reverse order.
- "Hora" derives from Aho-Ratra, the two-fold division of the day into light and darkness.
- Within each half, one sign is allotted to each of the five planets in the order Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — their order of distance from the Sun (Mercury closest, Saturn farthest) — counting away from Leo in zodiacal order and away from Cancer in reverse order.

Resulting lordships (derived from the rule above):

| Lord | Solar half (from Leo, zodiacal order) | Lunar half (from Cancer, reverse order) |
|---|---|---|
| Luminary | Leo — Sun | Cancer — Moon |
| Mercury | Virgo | Gemini |
| Venus | Libra | Taurus |
| Mars | Scorpio | Aries |
| Jupiter | Sagittarius | Pisces |
| Saturn | Capricorn | Aquarius |

## 3. Houses (bhavas)

- The celestial circle is divided into twelve equal parts called houses. The Sanskrit term bhava means "state of condition".
- Houses are a permanent division of space into twelve equal sectors of 30° each.
- Houses are not the same as signs: houses are superimposed on the signs.
- Houses in a horoscope start from the Ascendant and are named 1st house to 12th house.
- Astrologers map the signs and planetary symbols according to their positions at a given moment.

### 3.1 The Ascendant (Lagna)
- The Ascendant is the exact degree of the zodiac that intersects the eastern horizon at the time and place of birth. "Ascendant" means "rising upward"; it is also the name of the sign rising on the eastern horizon.
- The horizon is the line where the Earth or sea appears to meet the sky.
- As the Earth rotates on its axis, the whole zodiac appears to rise and set across the horizon, so every degree of the zodiac in turn rises on the eastern horizon.
- Timing: one rotation = 24 hours = 1440 minutes; 1440 ÷ 12 signs = 120 minutes (2 hours) per sign.
- In practice the Ascendant changes roughly every 2 hours, varying with geographical location and astronomical factors; some signs are exposed for longer than others (the source attributes this to the Earth's shape).
- In Jyotisha the Ascendant is called the Lagna, from a Sanskrit word meaning "tied down", "fastened" or "fixed": the Ascendant ties the positions of the planets.
- It is also called Udaya Lagna; udaya means "rising" or "coming up".
- Jyotisha defines specific lagnas for specific purposes:
  - Chandra Lagna (Moon lagna): the Moon's position is taken as the 1st house. This is the most common alternative lagna.
  - Surya Lagna: the Sun's position is taken as the 1st house.

### 3.2 The birth chart
- A birth chart is a sketch of the heavens at the time of birth as viewed from the place of birth.
- Three inputs are required:
  1. Date of birth
  2. Time of birth
  3. Longitude and latitude of the place of birth
- The birth place matters because the rising sign is taken as viewed from that place.
- Two steps: first calculate the positions of the planets; then present the results as a map of the heavens starting from the Ascendant.
- Planetary coordinates entered in the chart are geocentric (as if viewed from the centre of the Earth). The degree, minute and second of a planet in a sign is therefore the same at a given moment anywhere on Earth.
- House positions differ by place: when it is noon in Malaysia it is midnight in America, so the Sun is at mid-heaven in one and at the nadir in the other.
- The first horoscope cast after birth is the birth chart (natal chart), the main chart to analyse. In Jyotisha it is called the Rasi chart (D-1), Kundali / Kundli, or Janma Kundli.

### 3.3 Bhava Chalit chart
- The Bhava Chalit chart determines the house each planet truly occupies, relative to the actual Lagna degree.
- Bhava means house; chalit means moving. The chart shifts the house boundaries so they are drawn from the Lagna degree, not from the sign boundaries.
- When the Lagna is near the midpoint of its sign (about 15°), the Rasi chart and the Bhava Chalit chart are identical.
- The further the Lagna degree is from the midpoint of the sign, the more the two charts differ.

Two references in the Equal House method:
1. Lagna degree as the Bhava Madhya (middle of the house).
2. Lagna degree as the starting point of the house.

Method 1 in detail: each house spans 30°. The 1st house runs from 15° before the Lagna degree to 15° after it, and the other houses follow at 30° intervals. Some planets may then fall in the next or the previous house compared with the Rasi chart.

Worked example (Sripati Bhava chart, Lagna as the middle of the house):

| Point | Longitude | Sign | House in Rasi chart | House in Bhava chart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lagna | 5° | Aries | 1 | 1 |
| Sun | 28° | Aries | 1 | 2 |
| Moon | 355° | Pisces | 12 | 1 |

- 1st bhava spans 350° to 20° (5° − 15° to 5° + 15°).
- The Moon at 355° falls inside the 1st bhava, so it moves from the 12th to the 1st.
- The Sun at 28° lies beyond 20°, so it moves from the 1st to the 2nd.

## 4. Divisional charts (Varga charts)

- Divisional charts give deeper insight into specific areas of life by dividing the main birth chart (Rasi chart) into finer sections.
- A division is an orderly division of the 30° of a sign into parts called amsas.
- Charts built on the ownership of these divisions are called divisional charts or D-charts. The Rasi chart is D-1.
- All divisional charts are derived from the Rasi chart and are read in correlation with it.
- Each divisional chart has a specific use and a clearly defined focus.

## 5. Kalapurusha

- The Vedic rishis personified God as Kalapurusha and harmonised God into the zodiac.
- Kalapurusha (also Kaal Purush) is the personification of time as a cosmic man or divine being. Kala means time (also death, or black); purusha means man or being: "the Cosmic Man".
- It symbolises the eternal cycle of time intertwined with fate, death and celestial influences.
- Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, chapter 4: eternal time, which has no beginning or end, is identified with God — Time is God, the Lord of the Universe.
- As the hands of a watch indicate the divisions of time, the Earth's movement on the ecliptic is regarded as the Sudarshana Chakra, the wheel in the hand of Vishnu (the weapon of Janardana).
- The Kalapurusha Kundali maps the 12 signs, planets and constellations onto a human figure (kalanara), body part by body part, to predict health, fortune and life events.
- Aries marks the head, progressing to Pisces at the feet.
- The natural zodiac is also called the Kalapurusha chart.
- Medical astrology examines the horoscope in terms of the parts of the body. The right side of the body corresponds to the 1st through 7th houses, and the left side to the 7th through 1st houses (as stated in the source).
