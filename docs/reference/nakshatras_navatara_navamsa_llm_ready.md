# Nakshatras, Nava Tara Chakra and Navamsa — Jyotisha Reference

Scope: nakshatra fundamentals and rulerships, uses of nakshatras, nakshatra attributes and classifications (nature, orientation, guna, gana), the Nava Tara Chakra (Tarabalam), and the Navamsa (D-9) chart with worked examples.

Conventions used in this file:
- Nakshatras are numbered 1–27 from Ashwini. Degrees are within a sign.
- "Source note" marks places where the original slides were inconsistent; nothing was silently corrected.

## 1. Nakshatra fundamentals

- "Nakshatra" is explained two ways: naks ("sky") + shetra ("region") = "sky map"; or naksha ("map") + tara ("star") = "star map".
- The 360° zodiac holds 27 nakshatras: 360° ÷ 27 = 13°20' each.
- Each nakshatra has 4 padas: 13°20' ÷ 4 = 3°20' per pada.
- 27 nakshatras × 4 = 108 padas. 12 signs × 9 = 108 padas, so each sign has 9 nakshatra padas.
- One sign = 9 ÷ 4 = 2¼ nakshatras (two nakshatras plus one pada of the next).
- The Moon passes through the nakshatras in its monthly cycle. Its orbit takes about 27.3 days, so it spends about one day in each nakshatra.
- The 27 nakshatras form 3 groups of 9. Each group is a paryaya ("cycle").
- Planetary rulership follows the Vimshottari Dasha order: Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury.
- Counted from the natal Moon, the nine nakshatras signify, in order: birth, fortune, risks, prosperity, impediments, support, slaughter, friendship, thick friendship.

## 3. Uses of nakshatras

1. Fire rituals: nakshatras were primarily used to time religious fire rituals. The Moon's position was critical to the success of any ritual or sacrifice.
2. Birth chart:
   - The Moon's nakshatra in the birth chart is the Janma Nakshatra.
   - Newborns are named from the Janma Nakshatra: each nakshatra has specific syllables considered auspicious for beginning a name, so the name's vibration aligns with the native's lunar energy.
   - The Janma Nakshatra is used in prayers, rituals and remedial measures, as it carries the native's spiritual signature.
   - It determines the starting point of the Vimshottari Dasha system, which governs the timing of life events.
3. Vimshottari Dasha: nakshatras are the basis of the 120-year cycle. The planet ruling the Janma Nakshatra determines the starting Mahadasha.
4. Compatibility (Kundli matching): nakshatra matching is the traditional method for marriage compatibility. Dina Kuta, Gana Kuta and Yoni Kuta are calculated from nakshatras.
5. Panchang: nakshatra is one of the five limbs of the Panchang, used to determine muhurta, festival observances and daily spiritual practices.

## 4. Nakshatra attributes and classifications

Nakshatras are classified in various ways. Each has distinct qualities that influence character, destiny and auspicious timings. Each has a symbol, a Vedic deity and a shakti that give it its meaning and characteristics.

| Attribute | What it means |
|---|---|
| Symbol | Each nakshatra has a symbol. Symbols are the key to a nakshatra's hidden meaning; meditating on them unlocks its potential. |
| Deity | Each nakshatra has a presiding Vedic deity: the divine archetype or cosmic force governing its qualities. |
| Shakti | The unique power or capacity of the nakshatra — what it does, its functional energy in life. It has two bases: higher consciousness (the divine idea or cosmic principle from the deity) and manifestation (how that power shows up in human life). |
| Nature | The movement type of the nakshatra. Used in muhurta and to understand how a nakshatra expresses energy. |
| Orientation | Three types: Adho Mukha (facing downwards), Urdhwa Mukha (facing upwards), Tiryanga Mukha (facing forward or straight). |
| Guna | The activating motivation of the nakshatra: Sattva, Rajas or Tamas. |
| Gana | Deva, Manushya or Rakshasa. |
| Animal symbol and gender (Yoni) | Traditionally used to judge sexual compatibility between marriage partners. The characteristics of the animal resemble those of the nakshatra. |
| Varna | Brahmin (priests) — the source of spirituality. Kshatriya (warriors) — responsibility to protect the world. Vaishya (merchants) — responsibility to sustain and nurture through trade. Shudra (service) — responsibility to be of service. |
| Nakshatra Purusha | Each nakshatra represents a part of Lord Vishnu's body — the Kalapurusha. |
| Tridosha | Nakshatras are classified by dosha: Vata, Pitta or Kapha. |
| Tree | Each nakshatra is associated with a tree. A practical remedy is to nurture the tree of the nakshatra of one's natal Moon. |
| Direction | West, North, South or East. Helpful in finding lost items, from the nakshatra operating when the item was lost. |
| Colour | Each nakshatra embodies a vibration expressed through colour. Colours can be used to harmonise with one's birth nakshatra or to invoke a nakshatra's qualities (peace, creativity, strength). |
| Purushartha | Applies across signs, nakshatras and nakshatra padas. |
| Pancha Mahabhuta (five elements) | Applies across signs, nakshatras and nakshatra padas. |

### 4.1 Nature of nakshatras

| No. | Nature | Meaning | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dhruva or Sthira | Fixed, permanent | Firm in their belief system. They do not move much; once a decision is made it is hard for them to change and adapt. |
| 2 | Chara | Movable, ephemeral | Constantly moving, changing and blending into their environment. They evolve quickly and re-form to suit circumstances. In a fight they will probably flee. |
| 3 | Tikshna | Sharp, hard, dreadful | Domineering and very forceful in their approach to life. Can be impartial to the point of lacking empathy. |
| 4 | Ugra | Aggressive, passionate, driven, protective | Proactive, constantly modifying their surroundings. Forceful like Tikshna, but warmer and more emotionally charged. |
| 5 | Laghu or Kshipra | Light, swift, mentally acute, witty | An innate advantage in business and health. They tend to outsmart opponents in conflict, which makes their position highly auspicious. |
| 6 | Mridu | Soft, tender, highly sensitive | Artistic, empathetic, intuitive and emotional. Friendships and love are given great importance; sharing and communion are valued above all. |
| 7 | Mishra | Mixed soft and sharp | Combines Mridu's sensitivity with Tikshna's ability to destroy unwanted influences. Only two nakshatras: Krittika and Vishakha, both under the fire god Agni. Like fire, they can warm, burn, sustain or decay. |

### 4.3 Guna

- Rajas: motivation for fulfilling actions or desires. Represents the creative power in the universe.
- Tamas: motivation for inaction. Represents the destructive force required for a new beginning. Darkness: a state of ignorance, laziness, inaction, delusion.
- Sattva: motivation for harmony, elevation and purity. Represents the sustaining force in the universe.

Order of the gunas for nakshatras: Rajas, Tamas, Sattva. Each nakshatra receives three gunas, one per level.

- Level 1 — the 27 nakshatras are divided into three groups of nine:
  - Ashwini to Ashlesha: Rajas
  - Magha to Jyeshtha: Tamas
  - Moola to Revati: Sattva
- Level 2 — each group of nine is divided into three sets of three, again in the order Rajas, Tamas, Sattva:
  - Group 1: Ashwini, Bharani, Krittika — Rajas; Rohini, Mrigashira, Ardra — Tamas; Punarvasu, Pushya, Ashlesha — Sattva
  - Group 2: Magha, Purva Phalguni, Uttara Phalguni — Rajas; Hasta, Chitra, Swati — Tamas; Vishakha, Anuradha, Jyeshtha — Sattva
  - Group 3: Moola, Purva Ashadha, Uttara Ashadha — Rajas; Shravana, Dhanishtha, Shatabhisha — Tamas; Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati — Sattva
- Level 3 — within each set of three, the individual nakshatras take Rajas, Tamas, Sattva in turn.

## 5. Nava Tara Chakra (Tarabalam)

- An important concept for choosing the right time for activities and for understanding the relationship between nakshatras. It shows how favourable a day is.
- It relates the Janma Nakshatra to the day's nakshatra.
- The table is built from the Janma Nakshatra, so it differs from person to person.
- Tarabalam is always considered when determining muhurta.

## 6. Navamsa chart (D-9)

- The Navamsa chart is a minute analysis of the 9th bhava.
- Each sign is divided into 9 parts called amsas: 30° ÷ 9 = 3°20' per amsa.
- The D-9 chart is used to analyse marriage, the strength of planets, the spiritual path and more.
- Each amsa is designated Deva (god), Nara or Manushya (human) or Rakshasa (demon).
- The strength of the Lagna or planets is seen from:
  1. Exalted and debilitated planets in D-1 and D-9.
  2. Vargottama navamsa: the Lagna or a planet occupies the same sign in the Rasi chart and the Navamsa chart.
  3. Pushkara navamsa: "Pushkar" means to nourish. A Pushkara amsa is a specific degree of each sign that gives very auspicious results when a planet is placed there.
  4. Karakamsha Lagna: in the Jaimini system the planet with the highest degree is the Atmakaraka. The sign it occupies in the Navamsa chart is the Karakamsha Lagna. Planets in the 12th house from the Karakamsha Lagna are very important for spiritual progress; the deity represented by such a planet is the Ishta Devata.
