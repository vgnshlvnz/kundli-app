Scope: the nine planets (navagrahas) in Jyotisha — the Sankhya background, each planet's karakatwas (significations), chara karakas, and thirty classifications of planetary characteristics.

## 1. The nine planets (Navagrahas)

- Jyotisha considers only nine planets: Sun (Surya), Moon (Chandra), Mars (Mangal; Tamil Sevvai), Mercury (Budha), Jupiter (Guru, Brihaspati), Venus (Shukra), Saturn (Shani), Rahu and Ketu.
- Sun and Moon are the luminaries.
- Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn are the planets proper.
- Rahu and Ketu are the Moon's nodes (north and south nodes).
- All nine are referred to as planets (grahas) in Jyotisha.
- All nine have natural characteristics and significations.
- Karakatwa means the significations or areas of life a planet governs. Karaka literally means "the one who does" or "the agent". Each planet is a karaka (significator) for certain matters, indicating the roles, functions and influences it naturally represents in a horoscope. In Tamil this is called Karakathuvam.

## 2. Philosophical roots — Sankhya

- Sankhya Darshana is one of the six classical schools of Indian philosophy and underlies Jyotisha.
- "Sankhya" comes from a Sanskrit word meaning "number" or "taking into account".
- Sage Kapila is regarded as the father of Sankhya; the doctrines are compiled in the Sankhya-Karika.
- Creation arises from two eternal principles:
  - Purusha: pure consciousness, the eternal witness (male principle).
  - Prakriti: primordial matter or nature, the source of manifestation (female principle).
- Prakriti contains three inherent qualities (gunas):
  - Sattva: clarity; pure and good nature, illumination, happiness.
  - Rajas: activity; desire, longings, emotion, passion.
  - Tamas: inertia; darkness of the intellect, lack of desire, ignorance.
- Before creation the gunas are in perfect equilibrium (Avyakta, the unmanifest state).
- Creation begins when Purusha comes into contact with Prakriti. This disturbs the equilibrium of the gunas, and their interaction sets manifestation (Vyakta) in motion.
- The tattvas then evolve step by step: Mahat (cosmic intelligence), then Ahamkara (individuality or ego), then the mind, senses and finally the five elements.

### 2.1 Order of evolution of the tattvas

1. Purusha (soul, consciousness) and Prakriti (matter, nature)
2. Mahat (Buddhi, intellect)
3. Ahamkara (ego, self-identity), which differentiates through Sattva, Rajas and Tamas
4. From Sattva: Mind (Manas), the five sense organs and the five organs of action
5. From Tamas: the five Tanmatras, which give rise to the five Panchabhutas
6. Rajas supplies the activity for both streams

| Group | Sanskrit | Members |
|---|---|---|
| Mind | Manas | Mind |
| Five sense organs | Jnanendriyas | Eye, ear, nose, skin, tongue |
| Five organs of action | Karmendriyas | Hands (Hasta), feet (Pada), speech (Vak), excretory organ (Guda), reproductive organs (Upastha) |
| Five subtle senses | Tanmatras | Hearing (Shabda), touch (Sparsha), vision (Rupa), taste (Rasa), smell (Gandha) |
| Five great elements | Panchabhutas | Ether (Akasha), air (Vayu), fire (Agni), water (Apah), earth (Prithvi) |

## 6. Characteristics explained

### 6.1 Planetary cabinet
- Sun is the king; Moon the queen; Mercury the prince; Jupiter and Venus the ministers or advisors; Mars the commander-in-chief; Saturn the servant; Rahu and Ketu form the planetary army.
- Sun and Moon are allotted noble and regal qualities.
- Mercury carries a meaning of youthfulness, like a prince.
- If the Moon is strong and well placed and influences the factors representing friends in a horoscope, the person will have powerful or regal friends.

### 6.3 Gender
- Masculine: Sun, Mars, Jupiter. Feminine: Moon, Venus, Rahu. Neuter (eunuch): Mercury, Saturn, Ketu.
- Used mainly to determine the gender of children and siblings, and the type of qualities a person may have.
- Example: if masculine planets exclusively influence the factors symbolising the wife in a man's horoscope, his wife may have many masculine qualities.
- If the neuter planets are very prominent in a horoscope, the person may be slow to mature sexually.

### 6.4 Gunas
- Sattva (Sun, Moon, Jupiter): inclines one towards pure and noble thoughts, leading to virtuous and selfless actions.
- Rajas (Mercury, Venus): inclines one towards a sensual life in which active pursuit of money and passion is of utmost importance, leading to arrogant and selfish actions.
- Tamas (Mars, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu): inclines one towards mental or physical inactivity, punctuated by angry, violent actions.

### 6.5 Elements
- Sankhya holds that the whole of creation comprises five basic states of matter (Maha Pancha Bhutas), called tattvas because they are the most material of all the Sankhya tattvas.
- Fire (Agni, Tejas): Sun, Mars. Earth (Prithvi): Mercury. Air (Vayu): Saturn. Water (Jala, Apah): Moon, Venus. Ether (Akasha): Jupiter.

### 6.6 Natural benefics and malefics
- Planets are divided into Saumya (benefic) and Krura (malefic) by their general auspicious or inauspicious nature.
- Natural benefics: waxing Moon, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus.
- Natural malefics: Sun, Saturn, Mars, waning Moon, Rahu, Ketu.
- Mercury:
  - A natural benefic when not associated with natural malefics; otherwise a natural malefic.
  - Conjoined with both benefics and malefics, it shows mixed results.
  - It is the most mutable planet and takes on the quality of the planet it conjoins.
  - Not conjoined with any planet, it is a benefic.
- Moon:
  - From the 8th day of Shukla Paksha (waxing) to the 8th day of Krishna Paksha (waning), the Moon is benefic.
  - From the 8th day of Krishna Paksha to the 8th day of Shukla Paksha, the Moon is malefic and is called Ksheena Chandra.

### 6.13 Body constitution (doshas)

- Vata is air plus ether; Pitta is fire plus water; Kapha is water plus earth.
- Sun and Mars govern Pitta; Moon and Venus govern Vata and Kapha; Mercury governs all three; Jupiter governs Kapha; Saturn governs Vata.
