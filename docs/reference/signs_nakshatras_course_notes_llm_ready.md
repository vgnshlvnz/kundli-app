## 2. Kalapurusha — the zodiac as a body

The zodiac represents the body of the Kalapurusha (Time Eternal, the Cosmic Man). This conception is of great importance in the practice of astrology.

| No. | Sign | Body part | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | Cancer (Karka) | Chest / heart | Organs around the chest |

Rules:
- Benefic planets connected to a sign indicate proper growth of, or protection to, that body part.
- Malefic planets connected to a sign indicate improper growth of, or damage to, that body part.
- In an individual horoscope the body parts are also read from the twelve houses counted from the Lagna. The body part of the Nth house from the Lagna corresponds to that of the Nth sign from Aries in the Kalapurusha.

Worked example — Mars, Saturn and Rahu in Taurus:
1. Sign-based effect: Taurus corresponds to the face and neck region, so malefics there may cause weakness, scars or visible issues on the face.
2. House-based effect: if Taurus is the 5th house of the horoscope, it relates to the stomach and digestion, so the malefics may cause indigestion, acidity or other stomach disorders.
3. Combined: the native may have both facial issues (Taurus as a sign) and digestive problems (Taurus as the 5th house).

- The three qualities are also called movable, fixed and common (or dual); in Sanskrit Chara, Sthira and Dwiswabhava. As each has four signs they are also called quadruplicities.

## 7. Classifications explained

### 7.1 Odd and even, positive and negative
- Odd signs are positive: yang, active, outward-projecting. They suggest assertiveness, initiative and expressive qualities.
- Even signs are negative: yin, receptive, inward-drawing. They suggest sensitivity, receptivity and an inward or reflective nature.

### 7.2 Gender, Krura and Saumya
- Odd signs are male and Krura; even signs are female and Saumya. Krura and Saumya refer to malefic and benefic signs.
- If the Ascendant falls in a Krura sign and is masculine, the native tends to embody stronger, more manly qualities.
- When the Ascendant is a Saumya (benefic) sign and is influenced by feminine planets, the native acquires delicacy, grace, humility and a gentle, yielding nature.

### 7.3 Directions
East: Aries, Leo, Sagittarius (fire). South: Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn (earth). West: Gemini, Libra, Aquarius (air). North: Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces (water).

The directional classification shows which directions are favourable for a native. Uses, including in Prashna (horary):
- Travel: which direction is most beneficial or safe for a journey.
- Lost objects: the direction in which a missing item may be found.
- Work or residence: which location or orientation may bring success, harmony or prosperity.
- Personal favourability: whether the native's energy aligns with certain directions.

- Varna shows the natural disposition, tendencies and suitability of a native in different areas of life.

### 7.6 Body functions — Vata, Pitta, Kapha
- The Tri-Dosha (or Dhatu) theory is the foundation of the Ayurvedic concept of bodily constitution. Vata, Pitta and Kapha are the three fundamental principles on which the building and sustenance of the body depend. Dhatu literally means "supporter".
- In equilibrium they give health (Arogya); out of equilibrium they give ill-health and are then called doshas.
- Parashara's correlation: watery signs indicate Kapha; fiery signs Pitta; earthy signs Vata; airy signs mixed (Mishra or Sama) doshas.
- Charaka, in the Sutra Sthana, held that disease results from imbalance in these three functions: movement (Vata), transformation (Pitta), and lubrication and stability (Kapha).

| Dosha | Attribute | Elements | Signs | Function when balanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pitta | Heat | Fire and water | Aries, Leo, Sagittarius | Governs digestion and metabolism; produces hunger and thirst; keeps the body soft and supple; gives lustre to the skin, cheerfulness to the mind and sharpness of intelligence |
| Vata | Movement | Air and space | Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn | Sustains enthusiasm and vitality; regulates breathing; enables voluntary action such as speech and movement; drives circulation and the elimination of waste |
| Kapha | Growth | Water and earth | Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces | Smooth functioning of the joints; stability and structure; healthy growth and build; physical strength, reproductive potency, endurance and resilience; nurtures patience, courage and freedom from greed |
| Mixed | — | — | Gemini, Libra, Aquarius | — |

Application: each sign channels a dosha's qualities into personality and health. The Ascendant, Sun and Moon signs show which dosha dominates and predict strengths and vulnerabilities. Afflicted planets show where imbalance may occur; benefic placements show natural harmony.

### 7.9 Ascension of signs
Short: Aries, Taurus, Aquarius, Pisces. Medium: Gemini, Cancer, Sagittarius, Capricorn. Long: Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio.

- Used mainly to gauge a person's height, or the relative size of any organ or limb, taking into account the height of the planets involved as well.
- The 12 signs correspond to the 12 parts of the body.
- Example: Aries rising with its lord Mars in Pisces — both short signs — gives a small head. Scorpio rising with its lord in Virgo — both long signs — gives a large head.

### 7.10 Strength of signs by time of day
- Strong at night: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Sagittarius, Capricorn.
- Strong by day: Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Aquarius, Pisces.
- Ascendant and Ascendant lord in day-strong signs: vigour, courage and splendour. In night-strong signs: graceful and soft, as well as secretive.

# Part B — The three lagnas

## 9. Ascendant (Janma Lagna)

- The Ascendant (Lagna) is the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the exact time of birth.
- It is the most vital point in a birth chart: it defines the physical body, personality and life direction, and is the foundation for interpreting all other houses and planetary influences.
- Also called Udaya Lagna or Janma Lagna.
- The Earth rotates once in 24 hours, so all 12 signs rise in sequence in that period: about 2 hours per sign.
- The duration of each lagna varies with latitude and season. Near the equator signs rise more evenly (close to 2 hours each); at higher latitudes some rise faster and others slower. Precise birth time and location are therefore essential.
- At any moment six signs are above the horizon and six below. Example with Aries rising: below the horizon are Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra; above are Aries, Pisces, Aquarius, Capricorn, Sagittarius, Scorpio.

## 10. Moon sign (Chandra Lagna)

- The Moon rasi (Chandra Rasi, Moon sign) is the sign occupied by the Moon at the exact time of birth.
- It is central to understanding the mind, emotions and inner nature, and is often regarded as more influential than the Sun sign of Western astrology.
- It represents the manas (mind), emotional tendencies and psychological makeup.
- The Ascendant shows the physical body and outward personality; the Moon rasi shows the inner self, emotional responses and mental patterns.
- The Moon governs feelings, intuition and receptivity; the Moon rasi shows how a person experiences emotions and reacts to situations.

Uses:
- Daily predictions: traditional forecasts (Rasi Phala) are based on the Moon sign, not the Sun sign.
- Compatibility: the Moon rasi and nakshatra are crucial for emotional harmony in marriage matching.
- Spiritual growth: the Moon is linked to the mind's stability; knowing the Moon rasi helps self-awareness and balancing mental tendencies.
- Self-understanding: identifies emotional strengths and weaknesses.
- Decision-making: shows how one responds under stress or change.
- Remedies: mantras, rituals and lifestyle adjustments are prescribed from the Moon rasi to stabilise the mind.

## 11. Sun sign (Surya Lagna)

- The Sun sign (Surya Rasi) is the sign in which the Sun is placed at birth.
- It is associated with the soul (atman), vitality and the deeper sense of life purpose.
- It represents the self, ego, willpower and the guiding principle of life.
- As Surya Lagna, the horoscope is analysed from the Sun to assess inner drive, ambition and life purpose.
- It indicates core identity, ambition and the way a person seeks recognition.

# Part C — Nakshatras

## 13. Nakshatra fundamentals

- The 360° zodiac is divided into 27 nakshatras of 13°20' each (27 × 13°20' = 360°).
- Each nakshatra has 4 padas; each sign has 9 padas. A sign is 2¼ nakshatras.
- Planetary rulership follows the Vimshottari Dasha order: Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury.
- Nakshatras are associated with particular deities, symbols and shaktis (energies). Each carries qualities that influence character, destiny and auspicious timings for rituals.
- The 27 nakshatras form 3 groups of 9. Each group is a paryaya ("cycle").
- Counted from the natal Moon, the nine nakshatras signify, in order: birth, fortune, risks, prosperity, impediments, support, slaughter, friendship, thick friendship.

## 14. Uses of nakshatras

1. Birth chart: the Moon's nakshatra is the Janma Nakshatra.
   - It is used to name a newborn: each nakshatra has specific syllables considered auspicious for beginning a name, aligning the name's vibration with the native's lunar energy.
   - It is used in prayers, rituals and remedial measures, as it carries the native's spiritual signature.
   - In predictive astrology it determines the starting point of the Vimshottari Dasha system.
2. Vimshottari Dasha: nakshatras are the basis of the 120-year cycle. The planet ruling the Janma Nakshatra determines the starting Mahadasha.
3. Compatibility (Kundli matching): nakshatra matching is the traditional method for marriage compatibility. Dina Kuta, Gana Kuta and Yoni Kuta are calculated from nakshatras.
4. Muhurta (electional astrology):
   - Auspicious for beginnings such as marriage, travel or business: for example Rohini, Pushya, Hasta.
   - Inauspicious and avoided for major undertakings: for example Moola, Ashlesha, Ardra.

## 15. Nakshatra attributes and classifications

Each nakshatra has a symbol, a Vedic deity and a shakti that give it its meaning. Each is a unique vibration: some bring healing, others discipline, others spiritual awakening.

- Varna is a Sanskrit word meaning type, order, colour or class. Brahmin: priests, scholars, teachers. Kshatriya: warriors, rulers, administrators. Vaishya: agriculturalists, merchants. Shudra: service providers, labourers.
- Body parts: each nakshatra is linked to a part of the body, showing where energy flows smoothly and where imbalance may arise. Planets strongly influencing a nakshatra affect the health and sensitivity of those parts.

- Pancha Mahabhuta: nakshatras are classified into Prithvi (earth), Apas (water), Agni (fire), Vayu (wind) and Akasha (ether).

### 15.3 Guna

- Mahabharata, Ashvamedha Parva, sections 36–39, names the three qualities as darkness (Tamas), passion (Rajas) and goodness (Sattva). Tamas is the lowest, followed by Rajas, then Sattva.
- Rajas: motivation for fulfilling actions or desires; the creative power in the universe. The passion that drives one into action, often without the expected result, which leads to further action. The most active guna: motion and stimulation.
- Tamas: motivation for inaction; the destructive force required for a new beginning. It keeps one in ignorance, laziness, inaction, delusion and darkness. Heaviness and resistance, disturbing perception.
- Sattva: motivation for harmony, elevation and purity, irrespective of the results of one's actions. Knowledge, wisdom, calm and quietness of mind. Lightness, consciousness, pleasure and clarity.
- Everyone has a varying combination of the three gunas; whichever predominates at a given time is subject to change. The gunas relate to the planets, rasis and nakshatras.

Order of the gunas for nakshatras: Rajas, Tamas, Sattva. Each nakshatra receives three gunas, one per level.

- Level 1 — the 27 nakshatras are divided into three groups of nine:
  - Ashwini to Ashlesha: Rajas
  - Magha to Jyeshtha: Tamas
  - Moola to Revati: Sattva
- Level 2 — each group of nine is divided into three sets of three, again in the order Rajas, Tamas, Sattva:
  - Group 1: Ashwini, Bharani, Krittika — Rajas; Rohini, Mrigashira, Ardra — Tamas; Punarvasu, Pushya, Ashlesha — Sattva
  - Group 2: Magha, Purva Phalguni, Uttara Phalguni — Rajas; Hasta, Chitra, Swati — Tamas; Vishakha, Anuradha, Jyeshtha — Sattva
  - Group 3: Moola, Purva Ashadha, Uttara Ashadha — Rajas; Shravana, Dhanishtha, Shatabhisha — Tamas; Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati — Sattva
- Level 3 — within each set of three, the individual nakshatras take Rajas, Tamas, Sattva in turn.

## 17. Navamsa chart (D-9)

- The Navamsa chart is a minute analysis of the 9th bhava.
- Each sign is divided into 9 parts called amsas: 30° ÷ 9 = 3°20' per amsa.
- The D-9 chart is used to analyse marriage, the strength of planets, the spiritual path and more.
- Each amsa is designated Deva (god), Nara or Manushya (human) or Rakshasa (demon).
- The strength of the Lagna or planets is seen from:
  1. Exalted and debilitated planets in D-1 and D-9.
  2. Vargottama navamsa: the Lagna or a planet occupies the same sign in the Rasi chart and the Navamsa chart.
  3. Pushkara navamsa: "Pushkar" means to nourish. A Pushkara amsa is a specific degree of each sign that gives very auspicious results when a planet is placed there.
  4. Karakamsha Lagna: in the Jaimini system the planet with the highest degree is the Atmakaraka. The sign it occupies in the Navamsa chart is the Karakamsha Lagna. Planets in the 12th house from the Karakamsha Lagna are very important for spiritual progress; the deity represented by such a planet is the Ishta Devata.
